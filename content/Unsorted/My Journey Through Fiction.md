## No pictures? No, thank you

I grew up with a PSP®,Sony's first handheld console and the pinnacle of Japanese engineering. This mean that any form of entertainment you offered me was competing with the dozens of worlds I could instantly teleport myself too with that little console. Needless to say, I saw little appeal in books. Why spend time reading a story set in stone (or in this case, wood ) when I could live my own story on my PSP?

It would take something special to make me forego the pleasure of games , digital or physical. Enter: Jeff Kinney

## Greg Heffley

When you're in primary, you keep a mental note of who has what. Which kid has a good ball, who has the sharpener that actually works, who has the really pretty encyclopedia etc. Well, that extended to a very specific series of books. Diary of Wimpy Kid.

What started as a pass time between classes became something that stole my attention on the way home and even once I sat down in my room. They were so hard to put down.

The pass time turned into an obsession spanning 13 colorful books. Some borrowed, some bought, some downloaded on my dad's kindle account, all entertaining. Each readable in one sitting, and each joke still funny when repeated the next go around.

Each book followed Greg Heffley's journey through high school and all together formed a museum of his hilarious mistakes. They all started with Greg having a plan and then proceeded to Murphy's Law his plans in the most entertaining ways that created inside jokes that ripple across the internet to this day s

I loved and still do like that series (although I have fallen out of step with the new releases) because I could see that stories that didnt move on my screen were still worth sitting down for. It also was the first step in my journey from graphics to novels, but what sit in between genuinely changed my life
